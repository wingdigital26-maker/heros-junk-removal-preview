The basics

Price, timing, what goes and where it ends up.

How do I get a price?

Text a photo of what needs to go to (214) 277-9069 with your city. You get a price back by text. No form, no visit first.

What if I do not have a photo?

Call (214) 277-9069 and describe it. A rough list of what is there is enough to talk it through.

Can it go today?

Often, when the schedule allows. We run 7am to 8pm every day, so text early and ask for the soonest slot.

What do you take?

Furniture, appliances, mattresses, couches, hot tubs, estate and garage cleanouts, construction debris and yard debris. If you are not sure, send the photo anyway.

Where do you go?

Frisco, Plano, McKinney, Prosper, Allen and Little Elm. Nearby addresses are worth a text.

Where does it all end up?

The landfill is the last stop, not the first. Usable furniture and appliances go to local donation partners, and metal, cardboard and electronics go to recyclers.

Is there anything you cannot take?

Hazardous materials cannot legally ride on the trailer. If you are not sure whether something can go, send the photo and ask.

How does your pricing work?

Text a photo of what needs to go and we text back a firm price, before anyone drives out. If the pile matches the photo, that is the number you pay. Disposal is included and there is no hourly clock.

Do you provide donation receipts?

Yes. When usable items go to our donation partners, the receipt comes back to you on request. Families handling estates use this constantly.

Who actually shows up to my house?

The Hero’s crew in person, not subcontractors or marketplace strangers, and no surprises at the door.

Do I need to be home for a pickup?

Not always. Curbside and garage pickups can be approved by phone, with before-and-after photos when the job is done.

(02)

Same-day pickups

What typical means and when it does not apply.

Is there a rush fee for same-day pickup?

No. Same-day is quoted exactly like every other job, approved before we load. The one thing speed cannot change is household hazardous waste, which goes to a city collection program instead of on the trailer. The TCEQ guide for Texas households explains why.

What is the cutoff for same-day?

Call before noon and same-day usually works across most of DFW. After noon, worst case is typically the next morning's first slot.

Do you do same-day on weekends?

Yes, 7 days a week, 7am to 7pm. Weekend slots go fastest, so call early.

What areas qualify?

The northern DFW suburbs: Plano, Allen, Frisco, McKinney, Richardson, Little Elm, Prosper, and the cities between.

Furniture, couches and mattresses

Upstairs, inside, donated first.

How is furniture removal quoted in DFW?

The crew looks at the pieces on sight, whether it is one couch or a houseful, and gives you a firm number. Every job is quoted upfront before loading starts.

Do you take furniture from upstairs or inside the house?

Yes. We come to wherever it sits, including third-floor apartments, and protect floors and door frames on the way out.

Can my old furniture be donated instead of dumped?

If it is clean and usable, that is exactly what happens. We route donatable pieces to local charity partners first and recycle what we can of the rest.

Do you offer same-day furniture pickup?

Usually yes. Call before noon and we can typically be there the same afternoon, depending on route availability.

(04)

Appliances

Fridges, washers, dryers, water heaters.

Do you take refrigerators and freezers?

Yes. They go to certified recyclers who recover the refrigerant first, which is why they carry a small surcharge. Empty and defrost before pickup if you can.

Do you disconnect appliances before hauling?

Simple disconnections, yes: unplugging, water lines, dryer vents. Gas lines need a plumber's cap and hardwired units an electrician first; we take it from there.

How is appliance removal quoted in DFW?

A single unit gets hauled from wherever it sits, upstairs included. Fridges and freezers need freon recovery, which we handle. Multiple units are quoted firm on sight, always approved upfront.

Can working appliances be donated?

Yes. Clean, working units go to donation partners first, and we can provide a receipt on request.

(05)

Garage cleanouts

Sorting, timing, what stays behind.

How long does a garage cleanout take?

Most take one to three hours with a two-person crew, including loading and haul-off. Heavily packed garages run longer, and we give you a realistic window on sight.

Do I need to sort before the crew arrives?

No. Pointing works. Pre-sorting into keep and go areas speeds loading.

What garage items can you not take?

Household hazardous waste: paint, oil, antifreeze, gasoline, propane, pesticides. Your city's hazardous waste program takes them, usually free.

How is a garage cleanout quoted in DFW?

The crew walks the garage with you, sees what is going, and gives you a firm number on the spot. Always approved upfront before anything is loaded.

(06)

Estate cleanouts

Whole houses, handled with care.

How long does an estate cleanout take?

Typically one to two days for a three-bedroom home. The walkthrough sets a firm timeline before anything starts.

What happens to photos, documents, and valuables you find?

They go to a designated keepsake spot for the family to review. The crew sets aside anything personal-looking; you make every keep-or-go decision.

Can estate items be donated, and do we get a receipt?

Yes. Usable items go to local donation partners and we provide receipts on request, which many families keep with the estate's records. Old computers, TVs, and phones get routed the way the EPA recommends rather than buried.

How is an estate cleanout quoted in DFW?

We walk the house with you first, look at what is actually going, and quote it firm before any work starts. Nothing is touched until you approve the number.

(07)

Hot tubs, sheds and playsets

Draining, power, the pad.

Do I need to drain the hot tub first?

Yes, ideally the day before. We can pump the last few inches, but a full tub cannot be moved or cut safely.

Who disconnects the power?

An electrician kills and caps the circuit first. We take it from there. Electrical work stays with electricians.

Do you remove shed contents too?

Yes, quoted as part of the same job, or clear it yourself first if you would rather.

Can the concrete pad go too?

Pad demo is quoted separately because concrete has to be handled as heavy material. Ask during the walkthrough.

Yard debris

Limbs, brush, fence, storm leftovers.

Do you take branches, brush, and yard waste?

Yes. Branches, brush piles, leaves, grass, and hedge trimmings are all standard loads, loaded loose as they sit, and you get a firm number before we start.

Do I need to bag or bundle it first?

No. We load loose piles as they sit. Bagging or stacking speeds loading, but pointing works.

Can you haul debris from the backyard?

Yes. The crew carries or wheelbarrows it from anywhere on the property as part of the quoted price.

Do you take old fencing and storm debris?

Yes. Downed fence panels and posts, fallen limbs, and storm debris are all standard yard loads. Send a photo and we quote it upfront.

(09)

Construction debris

After the remodel.

What construction debris do you haul?

Cabinets, countertops, drywall, trim, doors and windows, carpet and pad, flooring, subfloor, fixtures, lumber, and remodel packaging. We load it from inside the house or from the driveway pile.

Do you take concrete, brick, tile, and dirt?

Sometimes, and it is handled differently. Dense material has to be handled differently from ordinary household junk, so we look at it before booking.

Can you come while the job is still running?

Yes. Plenty of contractors have us clear one phase of demo before the next trade arrives, then again at the end. Pickups run seven days a week.

Do you take paint and old chemicals from a remodel?

No. Paint, stain, solvents, and asbestos are household hazardous waste, and no licensed hauler can take them. Your city’s household chemical collection center accepts them from residents for free.

Do you clean up after loading?

Yes. The room or driveway is left broom clean. Metal and cardboard from the load go to recyclers rather than straight to a landfill.
