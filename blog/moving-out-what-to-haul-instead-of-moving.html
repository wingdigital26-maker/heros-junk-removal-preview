What to haul instead of moving

By the Hero’s team. Updated August 2026. A 7 minute read.

The cheapest box you ever pack is the one you do not pack. Every move includes furniture that will not fit the new place, a mattress nobody wants to sleep on again, a garage of things that came from the last move untouched, and appliances staying behind. Cut those before the movers arrive, not after. You pay to move volume, and the stuff you are not keeping is pure volume.

Most people work this out on moving day, standing in a garage at six in the evening with a truck already half loaded.

The three questions that decide what goes

Hold each item up against these, in order, and the decisions stop being sentimental and start being fast.

  • Does it fit the new place? A sectional that filled a Frisco living room does not fit a McKinney apartment. Measure the new rooms before you decide anything. This one question eliminates more furniture than the other two combined.
  • Did you use it since the last move? Boxes that moved once already without being opened are not going to get opened this time either. Same for the treadmill.
  • Would you buy it again today at what it would cost to move it? If the honest answer is no, it is not coming.

Anything that fails all three is on the cut list. What happens next depends only on what condition it is in.

The good stuff has a free route

Furniture in decent shape, working appliances, tools, dishes, and building materials all have somewhere to go that costs nothing. The awkward part is usually the carrying, which is what getting old furniture out of the house is about.

A Habitat for Humanity ReStore takes furniture, appliances, cabinets, and household goods, and many locations schedule pickups for larger items. Each store sets its own accepted list, so check it before you load a truck and drive across town.

If you want the local version of that conversation, where to donate furniture in DFW covers what the donation centers around here actually accept and, more usefully, what they turn away at the door.

We would rather point you at the free option and lose the small job than take money you did not have to spend. Where donation stops working is timing. Pickup slots book out, and a closing date does not move because a charity has a three-week wait.

Moving into a brand new house flips the same problem around. The cut you did not make before the truck loaded turns into furniture that does not fit the new floor plan, stacked in a garage next to the packing material, which is the call we get most often on our page about move-in clearing in Prosper. We also wrote up the boxes and builder leftovers after you close on a new build.

Book the cut before the movers, not after. Clearing out the day before the truck arrives means the movers only handle what is actually going, and the garage is empty when you do the final walkthrough. If you are selling rather than just moving, do the cut earlier still: what to clear before the listing photos works backward from the photo date instead of the move date.

What we take on a moving cleanout

Furniture that is not making the trip, mattresses and box springs, appliances, televisions and electronics, patio furniture, grills, exercise equipment, garage and shed contents, workshop leftovers, and the pile of boxes and packing material left over at the other end of the move. The furniture side of that work is on furniture removal.

Garages are usually the real job. A garage that has absorbed ten years of things nobody wants to decide about takes longer to clear than the entire inside of the house, which is why garage cleanouts is its own service rather than a footnote.

Mattresses deserve a line of their own, because they are the single most common thing people try to donate and cannot. Almost no charity in North Texas takes a used mattress, and most cities have specific rules about how one goes to the curb. We wrote the whole picture up in mattress disposal in DFW.

What we will not take, and where it goes

Paint, stain, thinner, solvents, motor oil, gasoline, propane tanks, pool chemicals, and pesticides are household hazardous waste. A move surfaces all of it at once, because the back shelf of the garage is where it has been sitting since the last owner.

We do not take any of it. Your city does, free, through a household chemical collection program. Plano residents use household chemical collection, and every city in the area runs an equivalent that we will point you to for your address.

One heavy item can hold up an entire move. If yours is a piano, a pool table or a safe, read piano, pool table and safe removal before you book the movers.

Using the city's system if you have time

If your move is far enough out, some of the cut list can go through the normal residential system at no cost.

Most cities around here run bulky waste collection on a schedule with volume limits and an accepted list. Plano publishes its rules at bulky waste collection, and McKinney covers its residential service at trash and recycling services. Read the accepted list before you stack anything at a curb, since a pile the city declines sits in front of the house you are trying to sell.

Recycling handles more of a move than people expect, especially cardboard, metal, and electronics. The EPA has a plain starting point at reduce, reuse, recycle if you want to route more of the pile away from disposal.

While you are working through the logistics, set the mail forward early rather than late. USPS covers how at standard forward mail. It has nothing to do with hauling and everybody forgets it until week two.

Timing it so the last week is not chaos

The sequence that works on almost every move:

Four weeks out, measure the new place and build the cut list. Three weeks out, book donation pickups if you are using them, since that is the piece with the longest lead time. One week out, book the haul for the things donation would not take. Two days out, we clear it. Moving day, the movers only touch what is going.

Reverse that order and you end up paying a moving crew to load a sectional onto a truck, drive it, and discover it does not fit through the new door.

Frequently asked questions

Should I clear out before or after the movers?

Before. Movers charge for what they load and how long it takes. Anything you are not keeping should be gone before the truck shows up.

Can you come the same day as the move?

Sometimes, though it is tight. A day or two ahead is a better plan, and it means the garage is already empty for the final walkthrough.

What about the stuff the buyer asked us to leave?

Leave it. Check the contract first, because appliances, window treatments, and mounted televisions are the usual items written into a sale, and hauling one off by accident is a problem at closing.

Do you take the boxes and packing material after we unpack?

Yes, and that is a common second visit at the new address. Cardboard is one of the easier things to route to a recycler.

We are moving out of an apartment. Is that different?

Mostly it is stairs and elevator access, which we plan for if you tell us the floor and whether there is a service elevator. Mention any building rules about move-out hours when you write.
