Office and small commercial cleanouts

By the Hero’s team. Updated August 2026. A 7 minute read.

A small business cleanout is a house cleanout with a deadline attached. The furniture, appliances, and electronics come out the same way, but a lease end date is real in a way a Saturday is not, and two categories of material have to be dealt with by you before anyone loads them: paper with customer information on it, and anything that holds data. Everything else is ordinary hauling.

Most of the offices we clear are small. Suites in a Plano office park, a storefront on a strip in Allen, a back room behind a shop in McKinney, a small warehouse in Frisco that a tenant walked away from. Plano generates more of this work than anywhere else we go, which is covered on the Plano junk removal page.

What comes out of a small office

The list is longer than people expect, because an office accumulates in the same way a garage does. Nobody throws out a chair, they roll it into the storage room.

Desks, credenzas, and conference tables are the heavy part, and half of them were assembled in the room they are sitting in. Task chairs are the volume part, because almost every office has more chairs than people who ever worked there. Cubicle panels and workstation frames break down into more material than they look like they will.

Then the rest of it. File cabinets, lateral and vertical, which have to be empty first for reasons covered below. Break room refrigerators, microwaves, water coolers, vending fixtures, and folding tables. Monitors, towers, printers, copiers, phone systems, network gear, and the box of cables behind the rack. Signage, shelving, and display fixtures out of a retail space.

And whatever the last tenant walked away from. Landlords call us about that more than anyone else does.

If the office in question is a spare bedroom rather than a suite, the job is smaller and the steps are different, and clearing out the home office after going back to the office walks through it.

The furniture side of that is the same work as a home job, which is covered under furniture removal. Break room appliances go out under appliance removal. What changes on a commercial job is the building, not the lifting.

The two things to handle before we arrive

This is the part that matters, and it is the part nobody plans for.

Paper with personal information on it. Old client files, employee records, applications, and anything with a Social Security number, an account number, or a credit report on it. Those do not go on a trailer. The FTC's guide for businesses on protecting personal information covers the disposal side plainly, and the separate rule on disposing of consumer report information is worth ten minutes if you ever pulled a credit report on a tenant, a customer, or a job applicant. Shred it or hire a shredding service. We will haul the empty cabinets after.

Anything that holds data. Hard drives, servers, copiers with internal storage, and old phones. Wipe or remove the drives before the machines leave the building. Once a tower is on a trailer, it is out of your hands, and you do not want that to be the moment you remember what was on it.

Do the data pass first, book the haul second. Shredding and drive wiping are the two things with a lead time. Everything else can be cleared in a single visit once they are done.

Where office equipment goes after pickup

Usable desks, chairs, and shelving go to local donation partners when they are in shape for it. A Habitat for Humanity ReStore takes office furniture, cabinets, and building materials at many locations, and each store publishes its own accepted list.

Electronics are their own path. Monitors, towers, and printers should not go to a landfill, and the EPA covers why at cleaning up electronic waste. We wrote the North Texas version of that in appliance and electronics disposal, which walks through what actually happens to a monitor or a fridge around here.

Metal from cubicle frames, shelving, and file cabinets goes to recyclers. Cardboard does too, and a cleanout produces a lot of it.

If your business is donating furniture with real value and you want to claim it, the IRS rules are at charitable contribution deductions. Ask your accountant, not your hauler. We are haulers, and we will not pretend otherwise.

Working around a building instead of a driveway

A commercial address adds rules a house does not have.

Loading docks have hours. Freight elevators have to be reserved, sometimes days out, and some buildings require a certificate of insurance on file before a vendor is allowed past the lobby. Property managers usually know all of this and will tell you if you ask, and it is much easier to ask in week one of a move-out than on the last afternoon of the lease.

Tell us the floor, whether there is a service elevator, and what the building requires when you write. That one message removes most of the surprises on the day.

Doing it without shutting the business down

Plenty of these jobs happen while people are still working, and it usually comes down to phasing or timing.

Phasing means clearing the storage room first, then the space nobody is using, then the last of it after the final day. The office keeps working and the pile never takes over the lobby.

Timing means doing it after close. We run pickups seven days a week, so a Saturday morning or an evening job is normal rather than a special request.

For a warehouse or a back room that has quietly become storage, the work looks much more like garage cleanouts than an office move. It is the same problem in a different building: years of things nobody wanted to decide about, stacked against a wall.

What we will not take

Paint, solvents, thinner, motor oil, pool chemicals, pesticides, propane tanks, and asbestos are household hazardous waste, and no licensed hauler takes them. Businesses have an extra wrinkle, because a company generating waste chemicals may fall under different rules than a homeowner does. The TCEQ is the state authority on that, and your city's household chemical collection program handles the residential side.

Full file cabinets are also a no until they are empty, for the reasons above. Tell us what you have and we will tell you plainly what stays behind.

How the job runs

You point, we carry, we sweep. A crew looks at the space, gives you a firm number before anything is lifted, and that number holds once the work starts. Disposal is inside it, so the quote is the bill.

Our own people do the carrying, not gig labor hired off an app that morning, which is why we are comfortable in an occupied office or a building with a property manager watching.

We work across Plano, Allen, Frisco, McKinney, Richardson, Little Elm, Prosper, The Colony, Wylie, and Fairview. Call before noon and the same afternoon is typical, though typical is the honest word rather than guaranteed, since access and job size are real variables. If you are working against a lease end date, say so. Tell us what you have and we will quote it before we load anything.

Frequently asked questions

Do you clear the whole suite or just the furniture?

The whole suite. Furniture, electronics, break room appliances, shelving, signage, and whatever the last tenant abandoned. Empty file cabinets are fine, full ones are not.

Can you work after hours or on a weekend?

Yes. Pickups run seven days a week, and after-hours commercial work is normal rather than an exception. Mention the building's access hours when you write.

Do you shred documents?

No. Shredding is a separate service and should happen before we arrive. We haul the empty cabinets once your records are handled.

What about the old servers and hard drives?

Wipe or pull the drives first. We will take the hardware once you have handled the data, and the electronics go to a recycler rather than a landfill.

Our landlord is asking for insurance paperwork. Is that a problem?

Tell us what the building requires when you write and we will sort it out before the day rather than at the loading dock.
